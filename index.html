The goal of Practical Assignment №1 is to implement a WebGL-based stereo camera system for rendering an analytical surface as an anaglyphic stereo image. The work is based on the surface model developed in the previous discipline “Visualization of Graphical and Geometric Information”.

The application renders the model in negative parallax using two shifted camera frustums: one for the left eye and one for the right eye. The left-eye image is written to the red channel, while the right-eye image is written to the green and blue channels, forming a red-cyan anaglyph image.

The model is displayed as a textured and normal-mapped analytical surface with a wireframe mesh rendered on top of filled polygons. This makes it possible to observe both the shaded surface and the underlying polygonal structure of the generated parametric mesh.

The stereo camera parameters can be changed interactively:
Eye separation controls the distance between virtual eyes;
Convergence defines the zero-parallax distance;
FOV changes the field of view angle;
Near clip changes the near clipping plane.

The model can be rotated around its center using the mouse. The implementation also prepares the rendering pipeline for displaying a webcam stream in the zero-parallax plane, which is required for combining real camera input with the stereo-rendered scene.

Drag the mouse over the canvas to rotate the model.

(u', v') = R(θ) · ((u, v) − (uc, vc)) + (uc, vc)

Keyboard controls allow modifying the texture center:
W / S — move the texture center up / down
A / D — move the texture center left / right

The rotation of the texture is animated over time, demonstrating that the shading and TBN basis correctly respond to dynamic UV transformations. The transformation affects the diffuse, normal, and specular maps simultaneously, producing coherent lighting across all texture channels.

Drag your mouse on the cube to rotate it.
(On a touch screen, you can use your finger.)

Interactive Controls

  • U segments — resolution along parameter u
  • V segments — resolution along parameter v
  • Mouse drag — rotate the model around its center
  • T — toggle stereo anaglyph / mono rendering
  • Eye separation — distance between the virtual cameras
  • Convergence — zero-parallax distance
  • FOV — vertical field of view angle
  • Near clip — near clipping plane distance

This assignment demonstrates an off-axis stereo camera implementation in WebGL. The scene is rendered twice with different projection matrices and color masks, producing a red-cyan anaglyph image.
